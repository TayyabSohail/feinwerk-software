IFRS 15 · Step 2, identifying performance obligations Cited in 42 findings across 11 clients

§22

At contract inception, an entity shall assess the goods or services promised in a contract with a customer and shall identify as a performance obligation each promise to transfer to the customer either a good or service that is distinct, or a series of distinct goods or services that are substantially the same.

Used in F-12 · Helios · Revenue policy v4
§27

A good or service that is promised to a customer is distinct if both of the following criteria are met: the customer can benefit from the good or service on its own or together with other readily available resources; and the promise is separately identifiable from other promises in the contract.

Used in F-12, F-07 · 2 clients
§35(a)

An entity transfers control of a good or service over time and, therefore, satisfies a performance obligation and recognises revenue over time if the customer simultaneously receives and consumes the benefits provided by the entity's performance as the entity performs.

Used in F-12 · Helios
Firm note

Bundled hardware and service. Treat the service element as a separate obligation unless the client can show the hardware is not functional without it. Flag any policy that recognises the bundle on delivery.
